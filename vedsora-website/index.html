The problem

An employee gets a payslip. A subcontractor gets a CIS statement. A musician gets nothing.

Freelance musicians are paid by many organisations, students and individuals, mostly by bank transfer and cash. Nobody issues a record of what each payment was for, so musicians and their accountants rebuild a year of income from bank statements.

For musicians

A bank credit shows a name and an amount, not who paid for which work. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is turning an annual reconstruction into a quarterly one.

For organisers

Temples, gurdwaras and cultural associations, often run by volunteer treasurers, rarely hold a reliable schedule of whom they paid, for what and when.

For accountants

Each musician client's income is rebuilt from bank statements and notes, with no link between a credit and the work behind it.

Core innovation

Three-way matching. Silence is never evidence.

Vedsora compares three sources for every income line: the original agreement, the payer's confirmation and the bank credit. The grade shows how many of them agree.

A
All three sources agree. The agreement, the payer's confirmation and a bank credit from a matching account.
B
The payer's confirmation agrees with one of the other two.
C
No payer confirmation. Counted in tax figures, but shown as unconfirmed and kept out of evidence totals.

Scope

Why Vedsora

The record has to start with the payer.

The facts that matter (who paid, for what work, on what date, under what agreement) exist when the work is agreed. They are lost by the time the money reaches a bank account. Better bank categorisation cannot recover them.

Quarterly digital reporting is arriving on fixed dates.

A low-income, fragmented workforce

The Musicians' Census found average annual income from music of £20,700, with 43% earning under £14,000 from music and a typical musician holding three to four roles (Musicians' Union and Help Musicians, 2023).

Payer-side records are becoming normal

The Construction Industry Scheme and, since 2024, the Reporting Rules for Digital Platforms show that a payer can be expected to issue a record of income.

Bank data with consent

Regulated open-banking providers can supply bank data with the account holder's consent, so a payer's statement can in future be matched to a bank credit automatically.

The market gap

Existing tools each hold one side of the payment.

Accounting, studio-management and booking tools do parts of the job well. None combines a record issued by the payer with reconciliation against the musician's own bank account.

Positioning

Studio software records what a teacher scheduled. Booking platforms record what was booked on their platform. Accounting software records what the bank received. Vedsora records what the payer confirms happened, proves it against the bank and shows how it was treated for tax, across every payer a musician has.

How it works

Three inputs, one match, a graded record.

Organiser statements, the musician's own imports and confirmations from past payers all reach the musician's Ledger. The matching engine compares the sources for each line and assigns a grade. Lines that cannot be reconciled go to a review queue.

Step by step

How each party takes part.

Organiser

Keep the schedule, issue the statement

  1. A treasurer creates an organisation profile with its charity or company number and an authorised signatory.
  2. They upload last year's payments as a spreadsheet, or add musicians as they are booked.
  3. Vedsora issues each musician a dated Engagement Statement and produces the organiser's year-end schedule.
Musician

Import, check, send

  1. A teacher or performer imports 12 months of bank statements as a CSV file, or lists students and engagements.
  2. Vedsora proposes a payer and income type for each credit. The musician confirms or corrects every proposal before anything is sent.
  3. Confirmation requests go to past payers in the musician's name. The musician sees a graded ledger.
Payer

One tap, no account

  1. A parent, student or organiser receives a link stating exactly what is being asked.
  2. They confirm, dispute or decline. No account is needed.
  3. An unanswered request leaves the line at Grade C. Where a learner is under 18, the parent confirms.

Design principles

Explainable line by line.

Deterministic rules

Every grade and treatment flag can be traced to its inputs and the rule version that produced it.

AI only assists

AI only helps extract dates, amounts and names from documents and suggest payers. The musician confirms every suggestion. No model assigns a grade.

Nothing overwritten

Changes are recorded as new events. When a rule changes, affected lines are flagged, not rewritten.

Portable by right

The musician owns the record and can export it at any time. It is never sold.

Pilot first. During the first months, Vedsora runs on pilot tooling: spreadsheet import, form-based confirmation links and manually issued statements. The first software release replaces it. Automatic bank matching through open banking is planned for a later stage.

Four elements

What is new.

I-1

Three-way match and grading

Income with a stated, checkable evidence grade instead of a self-declared figure. Accounting software sees only the bank credit; studio software sees only the booking; neither holds the payer's confirmation.

I-2

Payer-issued Engagement Statement

The organiser, identified by charity or company number, becomes the issuer of the record, as a contractor is under the Construction Industry Scheme, without any legal duty to do so.

I-3

Retroactive confirmation

A new user imports past income and asks past payers to confirm it through a no-account link, so the record carries history from the first week.

I-4

Classification provenance

Each line carries the tax-treatment flag, the rule and rule version applied, and the independent adviser's sign-off reference. Rule changes flag lines rather than rewrite them.

An honest claim

Known controls, applied where they have never reached.

Three-way matching is standard in corporate payables. Payer-issued statements are standard under the Construction Industry Scheme. Neither has been applied to informal freelance creative work, where the payer is a small charity and the payee a sole trader.

Vedsora does not claim to be first in any category. The mechanism is at design stage: specified, with the first-release screens designed, and now being tested in a manual pilot.

The matching, grading, statements and rules are deterministic. AI only assists document extraction and payer suggestion, because evidence a third party relies on must be explainable line by line.

Built to be tested

Thresholds are set before the results are known.

The central assumption is that payers will answer confirmation requests. It is being measured in the pilot, against pass thresholds fixed in advance. If payers do not confirm at a meaningful rate, Vedsora will not claim confirmation as the core of its innovation.

About

A UK business built around a record people can rely on.

Vedsora is a UK record-of-work platform for freelance musicians and the organisations that pay them, operated by Vedsora Limited.

Vision

Every piece of freelance creative work in the UK leaves a record that the payer issued and the bank confirms.

Mission

Give musicians and the organisations that pay them a shared, reconciled record of work, starting in the UK South Asian classical music community and extending to all UK instrumental teaching.

Operating principles

Five rules we build to.

The provider owns the record

Data is shared only with consent, can be exported at any time and is never sold.

Free to record, paid to rely

Recording and confirming are always free. Customers pay for schedules, statements, reconciliation and seats.

Silence is not evidence

An unanswered confirmation request never becomes Grade A or B.

Regulated activity sits with regulated partners

Vedsora holds no client money, gives no tax advice and supplies no credit references. Payment capture and bank data will come through authorised providers.

Rules are deterministic and signed

Every tax-treatment rule is versioned and reviewed by an independent adviser.

Business model

Free to record. Paid to rely.

Recording income and confirming payments stay free, because a complete record matters more than a fee for confirming. Customers pay when they rely on the record.

  • Organiser seats: for organisations that pay musicians
